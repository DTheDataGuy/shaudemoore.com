My Story

Just a girl from East St. Louis, Illinois, who moved to Seattle at a young age, never imagining where life would take her.

I

Where it begins

East St. Louis, Illinois

Shaudé’s story starts in East St. Louis, Illinois. It is the place she names first, every time, because it is the place that made her. Long before titles and stages, she was a little girl from a city that taught her how to be resilient, resourceful and proud of where she came from.

II

Growing up in the Pacific Northwest

A new life in Seattle

She moved to Seattle at a young age with her mom, a single mother, and the Pacific Northwest became home. She came of age in South King County and graduated from Decatur High School in Federal Way in 2008. Today she still returns to the classrooms that raised her, sharing her story so young people can see a path forward for themselves.

III

Motherhood, resilience and the will to keep going

A Season of Resilience

Becoming a mother at a young age brought both tremendous joy and significant responsibility. While navigating motherhood, Shaudé experienced periods of housing instability and financial uncertainty that tested her strength, determination, and faith in what was possible.

Those experiences became part of her journey, but never the definition of her future. With a daughter depending on her and a determination to create greater opportunities for their lives, she continued moving forward, even when the path was uncertain.

She earned her Bachelor of Science in Human Services in 2014, followed by her Master of Business Administration in 2016, and has continued pursuing education and opportunities for growth ever since.

That chapter of her life helped shape the leader she is today, one who understands that circumstances do not determine potential, that dignity matters, and that everyone deserves the opportunity to build a better future.

Her experiences continue to influence how she leads, serves, and advocates for communities, with compassion, accountability, and a commitment to ensuring others have access to opportunities to thrive.

IV

Building a corporate career

Sixteen years at Amazon

Shaudé spent 16 years at Amazon, growing into global program management, data center security management, operational excellence, organizational strategy and risk management. At Amazon Web Services she served as a Senior Global Program Manager, leading complex programs for stakeholders around the world.

Alongside her day job she led the people side of the company, too: President of the Women at Amazon Seattle Chapter, President of the Black Employee Network (BEN), Puget Sound Headquarters, and ultimately Global Corporate President of Amazon’s Black Employee Network (BEN), leading the network worldwide.

V

Leading, speaking and connecting across the globe

The world stage

Her work has taken her around the world to speak, lead and connect. She has led global teams, spoken on international stages and sat at decision-making tables she once could not have pictured. Each trip widened her view of what leadership can look like, and each one brought her back home with something to give.

VI

Advocacy that starts where she started

Coming home to community

Since 2018 she has chaired the Seattle MLK Jr. Organizing Coalition, the youngest woman and the first woman to lead it. She serves as Secretary of the Seattle King County NAACP and as its Southwest King County District Representative, as a Commissioner on the Seattle Sports Commission, as Board Co-Chair of the Technology Access Foundation (TAF), as a Board Member at Large for Speak With Purpose, and as CEO of the Central District Community Preservation and Development Authority, home of the McKinney Center.

She is continuing her doctoral studies in Organizational Leadership at Grand Canyon University, because for Shaudé the learning never stops.

“I’ve had opportunities to lead global teams, speak on international stages, advocate for communities, and sit in rooms I never imagined I’d be in. But no matter where I’ve traveled, what title I’ve held, or what I’ve accomplished, I’ve never forgotten where I came from.”

Shaudé L. Moore

What the journey taught her.

Your start is not your story.

Circumstances do not determine potential, and hard seasons do not get the final word. She wants every young person, and every young mother, to see that.

Be vouchable.

Trust is earned in small moments. Show up, keep your word and lift others as you climb, so people can vouch for you when you are not in the room.

Keep learning.

A bachelor’s degree, an MBA, a Cornell certificate and doctoral studies, with more to come. Growth is a practice, not a phase.

Today she is

a mother, a corporate professional, a speaker, an advocate, and a lifelong learner.
